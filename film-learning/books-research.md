# Reading library — research and interpretation

Edited 2026-11-07 (Asia/Shanghai, UTC+8). Independent bilingual paraphrase. New book and film pages use only original SVG schematics, not copyrighted web stills. Film readings and exercises are explicitly editorial, not claims that filmmakers used a given model.

## Bibliography and distinctions

- Campbell: *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949) is the principal monomyth text. *The Hero’s Journey* (1990) is also a conversation/biographical collection, not the same book. Three phases and seventeen recurring motifs are not a mandatory sequential checklist. The Foundation itself warns against that reading and addresses gender bias.
  - https://www.jcf.org/learn/joseph-campbell-heros-journey
  - https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1890731M/The_hero%27s_journey (the 1991 paperback record also lists the 1990 first editions)
  - https://www.jcf.org/product-page/the-hero-s-journey-joseph-campbell-on-his-life-and-work-ebook
- Vogler’s twelve stages are a screenwriting adaptation, not Campbell’s original twelve steps. The author’s public handout separates a middle Ordeal from a final Resurrection. *The Writer’s Journey* first appeared in 1992.
  - https://chrisvogler.wordpress.com/2023/11/04/a-handy-handout/
  - https://mwp.com/product/writers-journey-25th-anniversary-edition-mythic-structure-writers/
  - https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Writer_s_Journey.html?id=BKxFzQEACAAJ
- Aristotle did not prescribe screenplay acts or page percentages. Beginning/middle/end concerns a complete causal action; unity of action is not unity of hero or a mandatory time/place rule. Hamartia is not invariably an evil moral flaw; catharsis remains interpretively contested. The ancient text’s own historical prejudices are not endorsed.
  - https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/poetics.1.1.html
  - https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/poetics.2.2.html
- Syd Field’s *Screenplay* (1979) popularized setup/confrontation/resolution and plot-point diagnostics. A familiar 120-page / 30–60–30 illustration is not the same as Snyder’s 110-page illustration, nor proof of a unique direct lineage from Aristotle.
  - https://sydfield.com/guest-blog/utilizing-syd-fields-screenwriting-paradigm-to-understand-script-timeline-structureby-natalia-lazarus/
- Freytag’s *Die Technik des Dramas* (1863): five parts, three transitional moments. The original central Climax is not simply the modern late-film climax. The exciting force is treated as necessary in the source; the tragic force and last suspense are not both mandatory for every play. Five parts are not five equal acts.
  - https://briantriber.com/WritingSamples/Freytag_Drama/Freytag_11_Ch2_P2.html (public-domain translation, chapter II, part 2)
  - https://filmlexikon.uni-kiel.de/doku.php/p:pyramidenschema-3570
  - https://www.criterion.com/films/735-throne-of-blood (Kurosawa, 1957, Macbeth adaptation; our structure reading is separate)

## Modern Monomyth: explicit interpretation

The supplied label lacks author/edition information and does not uniquely identify a book. The site explicitly uses an editorial umbrella: modern screen adaptations and critiques after Campbell, anchored in Vogler, with Harmon’s eight-step Story Circle, Lawrence/Jewett’s critical American monomyth, and Murdock’s *The Heroine’s Journey* (1990). These are not interchangeable or a single official model. The American monomyth is presented as cultural critique, not merely a modern name for Campbell; Murdock’s gendered symbolic vocabulary is historically situated rather than biological prescription.

- https://screencraft.org/blog/how-screenwriters-can-use-dan-harmons-circle-theory-of-story/
- https://www.wired.com/2011/09/mf-harmon/
- https://www.eerdmans.com/9780802825735/the-myth-of-the-american-superhero/
- https://maureenmurdock.com/articles-the-heroines-journey/

## Film credits and the BRAVIA correction

- *Faces* (1968): John Cassavetes, writer/director; Al Ruban, cinematographer; original black-and-white 16mm production. Spontaneous-seeming performances must not be labeled wholly unscripted.
  - https://www.criterion.com/films/915-faces
  - https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/john-cassavetes-faces-indie-filmmaking
- *Pretty Woman* (1990): Garry Marshall, director; J. F. Lawton, credited writer; Charles Minsky, cinematographer. AFI’s onscreen credits and synopsis ground the plot facts; our class/agency/threshold readings are analysis.
  - https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/MovieDetailsPrintView/55709
- Verified Glazer BRAVIA work: *Paint* (2006), directly credited by production company Academy Films. *Balls* (2005) is Nicolai Fuglsig, not Glazer. The site makes no unsupported exhaustive claim about every advertisement in either director’s career; it does not confuse other Sony campaigns with BRAVIA.
  - https://www.academyfilms.com/portfolio/sony-paint
  - https://adage.com/article/the-creativity-awards/2007-creativity-award-winner-sony-bravia-paint/119101/ (award report describes the late-2006 debut; some full-text access is restricted)
  - https://www.luerzersarchive.com/sony-bravia-2005-balls/
- *The Zone of Interest* (2023): Jonathan Glazer, writer/director; Łukasz Żal, cinematographer; Johnnie Burn, sound; Mica Levi, music. British Council confirms year/premiere and credits; the Żal interview supports the remote, fixed multicamera method. The page explicitly separates studying form from aestheticizing victims’ suffering or imposing a redemptive hero arc on perpetrators.
  - https://filmsandfestivals.britishcouncil.org/projects/the-zone-of-interest
  - https://www.leitz-cine.com/interview/lukasz-zal-psc
